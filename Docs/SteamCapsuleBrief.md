# Hollow Siege — Steam capsule brief

What to generate, why it is shaped that way, and the prompt to paste.

---

## 1. Why this composition and not just "big skeleton in the sky"

The instinct is right: one hero against a power that holds the land. But a
giant looming over a landscape is the single most common dark-fantasy key art
there is, and on a store page it will read as stock. Three changes make it this
game and nobody else's. All three come out of the game's own fiction.

**The arms do not embrace. They occupy.** The game's own objective text is
*DESTROY THE CORRUPTION ANCHORS* — a region is held down by anchors around a
totem, and breaking them is how you take it back. So the Overlord's fingers
should drive *into* the land as those anchors: black spikes sunk into the
valley floor, one of them a totem with the shield-glow still on it. The pose
stops being a hug and becomes the thing the player spends the game undoing.

**The land dissolves into the map at the rim.** At the centre of the arch it is
a real place — the castle on its raised hill, fog in the valley, pine and
birch. Towards the fingertips it turns into the parchment map the game actually
has: ink hatching for the mountains, a hex seal, cream vellum, a dashed travel
road. That one gradient says *action game* and *conquest map* in a single
image, which is what Hollow Siege is and what no competing capsule will be
doing.

**The ember fracture.** The cinematic trailer already opens on a crowned stone
statue split by a glowing orange crack. Carry that exact signature into the
Overlord's bones and into the fissures in the ground. The capsule and the
trailer then look like one campaign rather than two commissions.

Two more things the fiction gives away free: **black ash falling** (Elias says
in dialogue that the falling ash *is* the Blight), and the fact that the
Overlord belongs on a **throne** — so he is not floating, he is seated in the
storm, which is a far better silhouette than a hovering figure.

### The geometry, and why it survives being shrunk

Steam shows the header capsule at 460×215 and the small one as low as 120×45.
The composition has to hold up at thumbnail size.

```
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │        skull + crown, storm-lit          │   ← reads as a shape
        │     ╭──────────────────────────────╮     │
        │    arm                            arm    │   ← the arch
        │   ╱     [ castle, fog, valley ]      ╲   │   ← the prize, warm
        │  ╱         LOGO SITS HERE             ╲  │   ← negative space
        │ finger──anchor        anchor──finger    │
        │             ▲ hero, small, backlit       │   ← scale anchor
        └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

At full size: undead king, cradled kingdom, lone knight. At 460 px: a crowned
skull, a glowing ring, a bright point at the bottom. At 120 px: nothing but the
logo — which is why the small capsule must be logo-on-a-colour anyway, not a
shrunk version of this.

The same image crops to the 600×900 library capsule by keeping the centre
column: skull at the top, valley in the middle, hero at the bottom.

## 2. Screenshots to attach as references

Image generators take reference images far better than adjectives. Attach these
from the ten you already took, and say what each is for — the wording is in the
prompt below.

| # | Screenshot | What it is carrying |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The cinematic trailer cover (crowned stone statue, ember crack, misty forest) | The palette and the ember-fracture signature. **Most important one.** |
| 2 | Shot 1 — the castle in the fog | The place that sits inside the arch |
| 3 | Shot 5 — the camp at evening | Warm firelight against teal night |
| 4 | The region map (parchment, ink mountains, hex seals) | The cartography the rim dissolves into |
| 5 | Any shot with the hero from behind, round shield visible | The silhouette at the bottom |
| 6 | Shot 6 — the horde fight | What the skeletons are shaped like |

If the generator only takes one or two, use **1** and **4**. Those two carry the
palette and the thing nobody else has.

## 3. The prompt

Generate it **without any text or logo**. AI text is always subtly wrong, and
you want the wordmark placed by hand anyway so it sits in the arch cleanly and
stays sharp when the capsule is downscaled.

```
Stylised painterly video game key art, wide landscape composition, dark
heroic fantasy. Rendered in the look of a flat-shaded low-poly 3D game —
clean readable shapes, simplified planes, no photorealism, no micro-detail,
no plastic render sheen. Painterly brushwork over hard-edged forms.

SUBJECT. A colossal skeletal overlord seated on a throne of black thorned
iron, high in a storm sky, filling the upper third of the frame. Only his
upper body is visible; the storm swallows the rest. A tall jagged iron crown,
half of it broken. Empty eye sockets lit from within by a dim orange ember
glow. His bones are pale grey-bone, weathered like old stone, and split by
thin molten-orange fractures that glow from inside, as if the skeleton is
cracked stone with fire behind it. Tattered black-grey cloth hangs from his
shoulders and shreds upward into the clouds.

THE ARMS. His two arms reach down and forward out of the sky, curving
symmetrically toward the viewer, enclosing the landscape below in a wide arch.
This is not an embrace — it is a cage. The arms frame the image like a
proscenium. His long skeletal fingers do not touch the land gently: they are
driven into the valley floor as enormous black obsidian spikes, half bone and
half monument, each one sunk deep into the earth with cracked soil and faint
orange fissures radiating out from where it entered. At the base of the nearest
spike stands a carved stone totem wrapped in a dim violet barrier glow.

THE LAND INSIDE THE ARCH. A wide fantasy valley seen from a high vantage:
low rolling hills, dark pine and white birch forest, a river, and on a raised
hill at the centre a fortified stone castle with towers and banners, small in
the frame. Thick blue-grey fog lies in the low ground between the hills,
glowing faintly where light hits it. A few tiny warm orange campfires dot the
forest. The valley is the brightest, warmest part of the picture.

THE DISSOLVE AT THE EDGES. Toward the outer rim of the arch, where the
fingers meet the ground, the three-dimensional landscape gradually turns into
an old hand-drawn parchment map: cream-coloured vellum, brown ink hatching for
the mountains, a dashed ink travel road, a hexagonal wax seal with a heraldic
sigil, faded compass lines. The transition is soft and painterly — real terrain
at the centre, drawn cartography at the edges, the two blending through a band
of fog and ash. Do not make it a flat map lying on a table; it is the world
itself fraying into parchment.

THE HERO. At the very bottom centre, on a dark ridge in the foreground, a
single small armoured knight stands with his back to the viewer, facing up into
the valley and the overlord. He is tiny — no more than a tenth of the frame
height — a pure backlit silhouette with warm rim light along his shoulders.
Dark plate armour with brass trim, a crested helm, and a round shield slung on
his back. He is the only human figure in the image.

ATMOSPHERE. Fine black ash falls across the whole frame like snow, heavier
near the overlord, catching the firelight as it drifts. Thin streaks of pale
lightning inside the storm clouds behind the crown. Volumetric god-rays
breaking through the clouds down into the valley.

LIGHTING AND COLOUR. Strictly three colours. Cold deep teal and navy blue in
the storm sky and the fog. Warm ember orange in the fractures, the campfires,
the eye sockets and the rim light on the hero. Cream parchment at the outer
edges. High contrast between the cold upper half and the warm lower half. The
brightest point of the image is the valley inside the arch; the darkest is the
foreground ridge the hero stands on.

COMPOSITION. Strong vertical symmetry. Skull and crown centred in the top
third. The arch of the arms frames a clear open area of negative space in the
middle of the frame directly above the hero — leave this area uncluttered and
relatively dark, it is reserved for a logo. Cinematic wide framing, clean
silhouette reading at small sizes, strong shape language.

NO TEXT. No letters, no words, no logo, no watermark, no signature, no UI.
```

**Negative prompt** (if the tool takes one):

```
text, letters, words, logo, watermark, signature, UI, HUD, interface,
photorealistic, hyperrealistic, photograph, 3D render sheen, plastic,
cluttered, busy background, many characters, crowd, gore, blood,
anime, chibi, cartoon mascot, oversaturated, neon, purple-and-teal gradient,
lens flare spam, motion blur, frame, border, collage
```

**Aspect ratio:** generate at **16:9 or 21:9 wide**, as large as the tool
allows. You will crop to 920×430 for the header and 1232×706 for the main
capsule. Generate a **second pass at 2:3 portrait** with the same prompt for
the 600×900 library capsule, or crop the centre column out of the wide one.

## 4. After it is generated

1. **Place the wordmark by hand** in the negative space inside the arch, in
   Cinzel — the game's own display face, already in the project at
   `Assets/DownloadedFonts/Cinzel`. Keep it clear of the fingers.
2. **Check it at 460×215 and at 231×87.** If the skull stops reading, raise its
   contrast against the sky rather than making it bigger.
3. **The small capsule (462×174) is a separate asset.** Logo on a flat dark
   ground with maybe one ember crack behind it. Anything else turns to mud at
   120×45.
4. Exact upload sizes are in `Docs/SteamStorePage.md`, section 8.

## 5. One honest warning

A painterly capsule sets an expectation the game then has to meet. Hollow Siege
is flat-shaded stylised 3D, and it looks good — but it does not look like an
oil painting. That is why the prompt pins the render to *"the look of a
flat-shaded low-poly 3D game, painterly brushwork over hard-edged forms"*
rather than letting the generator default to full painted realism. If the
result comes back looking like a Warhammer cover, push that line harder, add
the castle screenshot as a stronger reference, and lower the painterly weight.
A capsule that oversells the fidelity costs refunds later.
